Georgia & the Caucasus

Tbilisi → Kakheti wine country → Vardzia → Kazbegi. Ancient qvevri wine, sulfur baths, fortress roads, cliff monasteries, and the high Caucasus — the surprising former-Soviet contender, without needing Russia or Ukraine.

This is the most surprising of the four — the one that feels like a whole new chapter rather than another European loop. Georgia gives you an older wine culture than anywhere in Italy, fortress roads through the Caucasus, monasteries carved into cliffs, a layer of Soviet-era texture, and some of the highest mountains in Europe. It keeps the former-USSR thread Grace asked about, without needing Russia or Ukraine.

It is built around wine for Grace — Kakheti and its 8,000-year-old qvevri tradition — old fortresses and cave-cities for Michael, and mountain scenery for both of you. It is not a “standard Europe hotel breakfast” trip; it is cafés, courtyards, drivers, wine lunches, and landscapes that change by the hour. Stronger value than Italy, more adventurous than the Baltics.

The journey, day by day

Twelve Days of Wine, Fortresses & Mountains

Tbilisi and the ancient capital, the Kakheti wine country, the cave monastery at Vardzia, then the Georgian Military Highway up to Kazbegi.

1
Arrive → Tbilisi

An easy first night in the Old Town

Land in Tbilisi, transfer to the hotel, and take one gentle walk through the Old Town. Keep dinner simple — khachapuri (molten cheese bread), khinkali (soup dumplings), a fresh salad, and a first glass of Georgian wine. No heavy sightseeing on arrival day.

2
Tbilisi

Sulfur baths, Narikala & wine bars

Begin in Abanotubani, the domed sulfur-bath quarter — book a private bath if Grace likes a spa ritual. Climb to Narikala Fortress for the view, wander the wooden-balcony streets and Sololaki, and find the Leghvtakhevi waterfall hidden in the gorge. Evening in a wine bar or a modern Georgian dining room.

3
Mtskheta + Tbilisi

The ancient capital

A soft history day, easiest with a private driver: hilltop Jvari Monastery looking down over the meeting of two rivers, and the great Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, one of the holiest places in the country. Back to Tbilisi for a slow dinner. (If the trip needs to shorten, this is the first day to fold into another.)

4
Drive → Kakheti / Sighnaghi

Into wine country

Drive east into Kakheti, Georgia's wine heartland (≈ 2 hours). Sighnaghi is the romantic hill town — fortress walls wrapping the ridge, cobblestone lanes, and long views over the Alazani Valley to the Caucasus. This is the day the trip turns into wine country.

5
Kakheti · the heart

Grace's qvevri wine day

The main event for Grace. Qvevri wine — fermented in clay vessels buried in the earth, an 8,000-year-old method — tasted at Tsinandali Estate and around Telavi, with one long, unhurried lunch. Don't book more than two tastings; Georgian hospitality is generous and rushing this day would be the mistake.

6
Drive → Gori / Uplistsikhe / Borjomi

West, with Soviet texture

A transition day with optional history. Gori has the Stalin Museum if that interests you; nearby Uplistsikhe is a rock-hewn cave town carved into a hillside. If the museum feels too heavy, skip it and give the day to Uplistsikhe and the green Borjomi mineral-water valley, where you'll spend the night.

7
Vardzia + Rabati Castle

The “how is this real?” day

Michael's anchor. Vardzia is a vast 12th-century cave monastery cut hundreds of rooms deep into a sheer cliff face — chapels, tunnels and a frescoed church, all hollowed from the rock. Pair it with the restored, many-layered Rabati Castle in Akhaltsikhe. The weird, ancient, unforgettable day.

8
Return to Tbilisi

A needed reset

Georgia's road distances feel longer than the map suggests, so today is a deliberate reset. Drive back to Tbilisi, rest, eat well, and regroup before the mountain section.

9
Drive → Kazbegi · via Ananuri

The Georgian Military Highway

North on the Georgian Military Highway, one of the great drives of the Caucasus (≈ 3–4 hours with stops). Pause at the lakeside Ananuri Fortress and the viewpoints along the climb, then arrive in Stepantsminda (Kazbegi) and check into a mountain-view room.

10
Kazbegi

Gergeti Trinity beneath Mt Kazbek

The iconic image of Georgia — Gergeti Trinity Church standing alone on its hill beneath the glaciers of Mt Kazbek. Go early for clear weather, and take a local 4×4 or driver up rather than turning it into a hard climb, unless you both want the hike.

11
Return to Tbilisi

The best wine night, last

Drive back to the capital. Make the final dinner the best restaurant-and-wine night of the trip, and buy a few bottles or small gifts before you go.

12
Fly home

Onward from Tbilisi

An airport transfer and departure — usually with one connection back to the U.S. (Most Tbilisi flights leave late at night, so the last day stays relaxed.)

Where you'd lay your head

Design Hotels, Wine Estates & Mountain Lodges

Not a chain-hotel trip — character properties that are part of the setting: cool Tbilisi, a Kakheti wine resort, a walled hill town, a cave-view stay, and the famous mountain hotel.

Stamba Hotel

$$$
Tbilisi · design base

A converted Soviet-era publishing house turned design landmark — concrete, brass, leather and a plant-filled atrium, the heart of “cool Tbilisi.” Walkable to the Old Town, with one of the city's best cafés downstairs. (Rooms Hotel Tbilisi and Communal Sololaki are great alternatives.)

Lopota Lake Resort & Spa

$$$–$$$$
Kakheti · wine country

The most comfortable wine-country base — a lake-and-spa resort tucked in a green Caucasus foothill valley, with vineyards, pools, and long Georgian dinners. The restful way to do Kakheti. (Tsinandali Estate and Schuchmann Wines are the more wine-estate-formal options.)

Kabadoni

$$–$$$
Sighnaghi · town boutique

A boutique hotel inside the walled hill town of Sighnaghi, with terraces over the rooftops and the Alazani Valley beyond. Choose this for a town-based wine-country stay you can walk straight out of into the cobblestones.

Vardzia Resort

$$
Vardzia · cave-view

A simple resort set directly across the gorge from the Vardzia cave monastery — the on-theme choice, so you sleep beneath the cliff rather than treating it as a drive-by. Plain but perfectly placed. (Hotel Gino Wellness Rabati is the alternative near Akhaltsikhe.)

Rooms Hotel Kazbegi

$$$ · book first
Stepantsminda · mountain icon

The famous mountain hotel of Stepantsminda — a glass-walled lounge and terrace aimed straight at Mt Kazbek and the Gergeti church. Book early: the rooms with the view are scarce and the weather windows matter.

Rooms Hotel Tbilisi

$$$
Tbilisi · final night

Back in the capital to bookend the trip — a warm, library-and-leather design hotel in the leafy Vera district, an easy walk to the balconied old streets. Keep the last night simple, central, and well-fed.

Where You'd Eat

Georgia isn't a reserve-everything-months-ahead place, but a few meals should be intentional: one modern Tbilisi dinner, one big Kakheti wine lunch, and one final night.

01

Shavi Lomi

Tbilisi · Modern Georgian

A creative, art-filled courtyard restaurant — the perfect first “this is not normal Europe” dinner.

Order: lobio (clay-pot beans) · ajapsandali · house Georgian wines
02

Barbarestan

Tbilisi · Historic Georgian

Built from a 19th-century Georgian cookbook, resurrecting old recipes in a polished room — the dressier dinner.

Order: the historic recipe dishes · a glass of Saperavi red
03

Keto & Kote

Tbilisi · Romantic courtyard

Hidden up a hillside lane with a terrace and a glowing courtyard — the date-night fit.

Order: khinkali · grilled meats · a chilled amber qvevri wine
04

Tsinandali Estate

Kakheti · Wine estate & gardens

The Chavchavadze family estate — tastings, gardens and a museum feel; the strongest Grace anchor of the trip.

Order: a flight of Kakheti whites and qvevri ambers · a long garden lunch
05

Rooms Hotel Kazbegi

Stepantsminda · Mountain-view dining

Simple food, but the view of Mt Kazbek does the work — the scenic mountain dinner if you're staying there.

Order: whatever's local and warm, with the peaks framed in the window

Good to know

The Practical Bits

Getting there & away

There's no direct flight from the U.S. — expect one connection (often via Istanbul, Warsaw, Vienna or Doha) into Tbilisi (TBS). Judge tickets on total travel time, not just price. The whole loop begins and ends in Tbilisi, so a simple round-trip works.

The driver strategy

This is not a train trip. Book a private driver (or driver-guide) for the intercity legs — Kakheti, Vardzia, and the Military Highway to Kazbegi. It turns long mountain roads and wine lunches into something easy and safe. In Tbilisi itself you just walk and use Bolt taxis.

Safety

Georgia is welcoming and this is the normal tourist loop. The one firm rule: stay clear of Abkhazia and South Ossetia, the two breakaway regions the U.S. State Department warns against — this itinerary doesn't go near either. Use hotel-arranged transfers or vetted drivers for intercity moves.

Best time to go

May–June and September are ideal. July still works: warm and lively in Tbilisi and Kakheti, cooler up in Kazbegi. Mountain weather changes fast, so build in flexible time and go up to Gergeti on the first clear morning you get.

Weather & packing

Heat in the lowlands and cool evenings at altitude — pack for both. Bring comfortable walking shoes: the old towns, cave sites and fortress steps are uneven and steep in places.

What to book first

Mountain-view rooms at Rooms Hotel Kazbegi, your Kakheti wine-country hotel, and the on-theme Vardzia stay all have few good options and sell out in summer — lock those plus the driver early. Reserve a couple of Kakheti tastings and the big Tbilisi dinners; leave the rest loose.

Food & dietary notes

Georgian food is generous and wine-forward, but it leans on bread, cheese and walnut-rich sauces. Flag dietary needs ahead at wine-country meals, where menus are less flexible than in the city.

Loved for

The most surprising of the four — ancient wine, fortress roads, cliff monasteries and the high Caucasus, with strong value on the ground and unforgettable long lunches.

The trade-off

The most adventurous logistically — longer flights, mountain roads, and more trust in drivers and local hotels than Western Europe asks for.
