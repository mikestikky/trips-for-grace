Northern Italy & Slovenia

Turin → the Langhe wine hills → Venice → the Dolomites → Lake Bled → Ljubljana. A slow, indulgent arc of wine, mountains, and Venetian romance — built around your love of Italy.

This is a slow, indulgent arc from the elegant arcades of Piedmont to the emerald shores of the Julian Alps — a journey stitched together by wine, water, and mountains. It begins in Turin's chocolate-and-vermouth cafés, drifts into the rolling, UNESCO-listed hills of the Langhe where Barolo and Moscato are born, then crosses east to Venice for two nights of pure romance before climbing into the cathedral-spire peaks of the Dolomites.

The final act crosses into Slovenia, where a fairy-tale island floats on Lake Bled and Ljubljana's dragon-guarded riverfront glows at dusk. It's built for couples who measure a trip in long lunches, golden-hour drives, quiet canals at dawn, and views that make you forget to check your phone.

The journey, day by day

Twelve Nights of Wine, Venice & Mountains

Two nights in Turin, three in the Langhe, two of Venetian romance, three in the Dolomites, then over the border to Slovenia.

1
Arrive → Turin

Baroque grandeur & vermouth twilight

Fly into Turin, or land in Milan and glide over by fast train in about an hour. Keep the first night easy: wander the porticoes of Via Roma into the ceremonial Piazza San Carlo — the city's “drawing room” — for an aperitivo of Turin-born vermouth, then an unhurried early dinner.

2
Turin

Royal city, Mole & Egyptian treasures

Ride the lift inside the Mole Antonelliana, Turin's improbable spire, then the Museo Egizio — the most important Egyptian collection outside Cairo. Stroll Piazza Castello and the Royal Palace, ducking into a café for bicerin, the city's layered espresso-chocolate-cream drink. Dinner at the gilded 18th-century Del Cambio.

3
Drive → the Langhe

Hilltop castles & first vines

Collect the car and drive ≈ 90 minutes south into the Langhe, the wave-like wine hills now under UNESCO protection. This is the Grace anchor. Check into Casa di Langa, then orient at La Morra's belvedere — the postcard sweep across the Barolo hills — and the candy-striped Cappella del Barolo, with a first Moscato or Barolo as the sun drops.

4
Langhe · the heart

A full wine day, taken slowly

Not a checklist — a slow day. A Moscato d'Asti tasting near Canelli or Asti, then a Barolo or Barbaresco for contrast, a long lunch at Osteria dell'Arco in Alba (or La Ciau del Tornavento), and an afternoon of hill-town wandering. Most cellars are by appointment — book ahead.

5
Langhe · the buffer day

Cellars, medieval villages & sunset

The day to keep — the one that makes the whole trip feel romantic instead of logistical. Tour Canelli's underground “cathedral” wine cellars, wander the perfectly preserved medieval villages of Neive and Barbaresco, stop for hazelnut and chocolate, and end with a golden-hour aperitivo over the vines.

6
Langhe → Venice

Across to the lagoon

A longer movement day. For romance, return the car in Turin and take a high-speed train to Venice (≈ 3 hr 10 min) — arriving by rail and stepping straight into the lagoon is part of the magic. (Or drive to the edge, park before the city, and enter by water.) Evening: check in, no museums — a walk through Cannaregio or Dorsoduro, a cicchetti dinner, and a late canal stroll once the day-trippers have gone.

7
Venice · the romance day

A city of mornings and nights

Treat Venice as a city of mornings and nights, not midday sightseeing. Early: San Marco before the crowds, the Rialto Market, quiet canal wandering. Midday: hide from the heat — a long lunch, a Dorsoduro museum (the Accademia or Peggy Guggenheim), a café break. Evening: a gondola or private water-taxi glide if you want the splurge, a cicchetti crawl, and a walk after dark.

8
Venice → the Dolomites

Alpine ascent to Val Gardena

Pick up a car after Venice (or take a private transfer) and drive ≈ 3 hours north, trading the lagoon for vertical limestone — the “Pale Mountains” that glow rose-pink at sunset. Settle into the Adler Spa Resort in Ortisei and spend the afternoon in the thermal pools as the peaks turn gold, with a first taste of South Tyrolean cooking.

9
Dolomites · Seceda

The tilted spires of the Seceda ridge

One of the biggest “wow” days. Ride the lifts to the Seceda ridgeline and its dramatic tilted spires for an easy, jaw-dropping ridge walk. Lunch is a rifugio affair — bread dumplings, barley soup, apple strudel — then an afternoon spa soak back at the hotel.

10
Dolomites · slow mountain day

Alpe di Siusi & cooling off

Keep it flexible — this is the cooling-off portion after Venice. Cable-car up to the Alpe di Siusi, Europe's largest high meadow, for blissful walking beneath the Sassolungo massif, or take a scenic drive, a long lunch, and an afternoon wandering and shopping in Ortisei.

11
Drive → Lake Bled

Over the border into Slovenia

A real travel day, but a beautiful one — southeast through the Julian Alps into Slovenia, arriving at Vila Bled, Tito's former lakeside summer residence. A first walk along the shore as the island church floats into view, then dinner with a view. Bled is busy in July — staying overnight lets you have it early and late.

12
Bled → Ljubljana · the finale

Island church, then dragons & a castle table

A morning in Bled: a pletna boat to the island, the 99 steps and the wishing bell, clifftop Bled Castle for the definitive panorama and a slice of kremšnita cream cake (Vintgar Gorge too, if timed early and seasonal). Then a 40-minute drive to Ljubljana — the Triple Bridge, the Dragon Bridge, a riverside dinner, and the funicular up to end the journey at Strelec, in the castle's Archer's Tower.

13
Fly home

Onward from Ljubljana

Depart from Ljubljana for the cleanest route fit, or from Venice or Milan, which often have better airfare. An open-jaw — in via Milan/Turin, out via Ljubljana/Venice — saves the most backtracking.

Where you'd lay your head

Inns, Palazzi & Alpine Spas

A Belle Époque townhouse, a vineyard resort, a Grand Canal palazzo, an alpine spa, and a president's former villa.

Opera35

$$–$$$
Turin

A 19th-century Belle Époque building in the leafy, café-lined San Salvario quarter near the Po. Frescoes and stucco survive alongside crisp contemporary design, with a hidden courtyard for rare city-centre quiet. Intimate and unmistakably not a chain.

Casa di Langa

$$$$
Langhe wine country · most romantic

A contemporary luxury resort on a 100-hectare estate of its own vineyards and hazelnut groves in the heart of Barolo country. Infinity pool, spa, and the Michelin-recognized Fàula restaurant, with floor-to-ceiling windows framing the vine-striped horizon.

Palazzo Stern

$$$–$$$$
Venice · Dorsoduro

A neo-Gothic palazzo right on the Grand Canal in romantic, less-frantic Dorsoduro — mosaic floors, a marble staircase and a waterside terrace for watching the boats drift by. Close to the Accademia and Peggy Guggenheim, but a world away from the San Marco crush.

Adler Spa Resort Dolomiti

$$$$
Ortisei, Val Gardena

An expansive alpine spa resort with the Sassolungo and Seceda peaks as backdrop. The draw is the spa — indoor-outdoor thermal pools and saunas with mountain views — plus warm Tyrolean rooms and a half-board larder of Italian-Austrian cooking.

Vila Bled

$$$–$$$$
Lake Bled

The former summer residence of Yugoslav president Tito, in a lakeside park with a private beach and direct views to Bled Island. History soaks the walls — Khrushchev, Nasser and Indira Gandhi were all guests — with a unique, cinematic period aura.

Zlata Ladjica

$$–$$$
Ljubljana

The “Golden Little Boat,” a small boutique right on the Ljubljanica river in the pedestrian old town, steps from the Triple Bridge. Intimate and warmly designed — the ideal final-night base, walkable to everything.

Tables worth booking

Where You'd Eat

Piedmont's truffle-and-Barolo cooking, a Venetian bacaro crawl, a mountain hut, and a tasting menu in a castle tower.

01

Del Cambio

Turin · Piedmontese fine dining

A gilded 18th-century landmark where Cavour himself dined — now Michelin-starred, the historic salons gloriously intact.

Order: vitello tonnato · agnolotti del plin · finanziera · a glass of Barolo
02

Osteria dell'Arco

Alba · Traditional Langhe

Run by the team behind the birthplace of Slow Food — honest, regional cooking on Alba's main piazza.

Order: tajarin with butter (and truffle in season) · brasato al Barolo · carne cruda
03

Cantina Do Spade & the bacari

Venice · Cicchetti & ombre

A centuries-old bacaro tucked behind the Rialto Market — the way to eat Venice. Crawl from one to the next (Vini da Gigio and Osteria Anice Stellato in Cannaregio too), small plate by small plate.

Order: cicchetti — baccalà mantecato, fried sarde in saor, a spritz or an ombra of wine
04

Mountain Rifugios

Dolomites · South Tyrolean alpine

Sun-deck huts on the Alpe di Siusi and beneath Sassolungo serve hearty, view-drenched lunches mid-hike.

Order: canederli (speck dumplings) · barley soup · Kaiserschmarrn & apple strudel
05

Strelec

Ljubljana · Modern Slovenian tasting menu

Set in Ljubljana Castle's round Archer's Tower with sweeping city views — chef Igor Jagodic's showpiece. The perfect last supper.

Order: the chef's tasting menu (closed Sundays · reserve well ahead)

The Practical Bits

Getting there & away

Fly into Turin, or into Milan and take a fast train to Torino Porta Nuova (≈ 1 hr). Depart from Ljubljana — or from Venice or Milan if airfare is better. An open-jaw (in via Milan/Turin, out via Ljubljana) avoids backtracking.

The car strategy

Two short rentals beat one long one. Rent in Turin for the Langhe and return it before Venice; pick up a fresh car after Venice for the Dolomites → Slovenia leg, with a one-way drop in Ljubljana (confirm cross-border permission at booking).

Don't drive in Venice

Venice is car-free. Either return the car before you arrive and take the train in, or park at Tronchetto / Piazzale Roma and continue by vaporetto or water taxi. Don't keep a car while staying in the city.

Venice in July

It will be busy. Make it work by staying overnight, doing early mornings and late evenings, and avoiding San Marco / Rialto midday. Venice's day-tripper access fee applies on set high-traffic dates; overnight guests are generally exempt but may still need to register for a QR code — check before you go.

Driving & road notes

Turin's historic centre has ZTL camera zones — park outside and walk, or have the hotel register your plate. Buy a Slovenian motorway vinjeta sticker. The Dolomite passes are gorgeous but winding — drive them by day.

Best time to go

Mid-June to early September is the sweet spot, when the Dolomites' hiking season is at its peak — lifts running, rifugios open, meadows in bloom. Venice and Bled are busiest in July (lean on early/late timing); September brings harvest energy and thinner crowds.

Book early

The bottlenecks all sell out in high summer: Venice hotels, the Langhe winery visits, Strelec, and Casa di Langa / Adler Spa. Reserve the headline stays and tables weeks ahead.

Loved for

Romance, world-class scenery, and the best food and wine of the three — Venice, wine country, and the Alps in one trip. The one you'd plan for a once-in-a-while celebration.

The trade-off

The most expensive and the busiest in peak summer, and the most logistics — two rentals, ZTL zones, Venice's car-free access, and by-appointment bookings.
